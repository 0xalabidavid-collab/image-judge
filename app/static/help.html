What this tool is for

An AI image editor was asked to do something (the request) and produced two results, A and B. This tool asks an AI judge which result is the better response to the request. You are the teacher: you check its answer, and when it is wrong or unsure you say why. It learns from what you write, so your reasons matter more than the clicking.

Your routine (on the Judge page)

  1. Paste the request into the Prompt box.
  2. Add the images: the original image(s), then Result A, then Result B. Paste with Ctrl+V (it fills the highlighted box), drag and drop, or click a box.
  3. Press Evaluate and wait about a minute.
  4. Mark it. Decide for yourself which result is better, using your project's labelling guidelines, then:
    • The judge picked the right one and was Confident: press Correct. Done.
    • It picked the wrong one: press Wrong and write one sentence saying why the other result is better.
    • It was not sure but you know the answer: press A is correct or B is correct and write why.
    • You genuinely cannot tell: press I'm not sure. Nothing is saved.

Writing a good reason

One specific sentence about what you can see. Say what is wrong in the loser and what is right in the winner.

Good: A still shows the old logo in the corner; B replaced it with the new one as asked.
Good: B has six fingers on the left hand; A's hands look normal and it did the requested colour change.
Not helpful: B is better. / A looks bad. / I prefer B.

Only write what you are sure of. A wrong reason teaches it something wrong, for everyone.

What the words mean

Confident
Every check agreed, in both image orders. Still check it, since it can be confidently wrong.
Review
Most checks agreed but not all. Treat it as a leaning, not an answer.
Unclear
The checks disagreed, so it gives no verdict rather than guessing. Choose the answer yourself.
Your answer: "—"
You have not marked that evaluation yet. Open it from the list to mark it.
Lessons
Rules the judge has learned from corrections. New lessons are tested on other tasks before they are used.

The other pages

  • Lessons: what it has learned, who taught it, and whether each new lesson passed its test. It also lists your earlier answers that still need a reason. Adding those is very helpful.
  • Train & Test and Benchmark: for the project owner. They run big batches and can change which lessons are in use for everyone, so leave them alone unless you were asked to use them.

Good to know

  • Your answers are saved with your email, so the owner can see who taught what.
  • If two people mark the same kind of task differently, it gets confused. When in doubt, follow the written labelling guidelines, not personal taste.
  • If something looks broken, note the evaluation number (like #104) and tell the owner.
